Key Price Points

Your data-driven pricing recommendations based on customer feedback

Floor Price (PMC)
-
Your minimum credible price. Below this, too many people question quality.
Optimal Price (OPP)
-
The price with the least total resistance. Note: "optimal" means least pushback, not maximum revenue.
Market Price (IPP)
-
Equal shares call it a bargain and expensive. The price consumers would expect to see on the shelf.
Ceiling Price (PME)
-
Your absolute maximum. Beyond this, you lose more people than you could possibly attract.
Acceptable Price Range
-
The range between Floor and Ceiling. Your pricing decision should live somewhere in this band.

Price pointPriceWhere the curves crossWhy it has this name
Floor
Point of Marginal Cheapness (PMC)		-Too Cheap meets ExpensiveThe edge of “too cheap”: below it, more people call the price too cheap than call it expensive.
Optimal
Optimal Price Point (OPP)		-Too Cheap meets Too ExpensiveAs many people reject the price as too cheap as reject it as too expensive: the least resistance.
Market
Indifference Price Point (IPP)		-Bargain meets ExpensiveAs many people call the price a bargain as call it expensive: buyers are indifferent, so it is the price they expect to see.
Ceiling
Point of Marginal Expensiveness (PME)		-Bargain meets Too ExpensiveThe edge of “too expensive”: above it, more people call the price too expensive than call it a bargain.

The shaded band, from the Floor to the Ceiling, is the acceptable price range.

## Corrections and adaptations

- The supplied animal test diagram's “ribosomes” pointer lands on the ER drawing. Its nucleus pointer lands near the nucleolus. The website uses the existing authored, independently tested cell model instead. All eight new label questions have a numbered marker and an accessible feature description. The existing complete plant/animal labeling labs retain drag, tap and keyboard alternatives and explanations.
- The source notes' prokaryote fill-in wording is incomplete and imprecise about membrane-bound structures. The website states: prokaryotes lack a membrane-bound nucleus; both cell types have DNA, ribosomes, cytoplasm and a membrane. Bacteria and archaea are examples of prokaryotes.
- The class inquiry uses 1673 for Leeuwenhoek and 1858 for Virchow. The new summary uses “1670s” for Leeuwenhoek's living-microbe observations and retains the class's 1858 Virchow date without portraying either as a single decisive invention/discovery date. The existing Concept 1 experience is preserved. Janssen attribution is explicitly uncertain; Brown and Chatton remain secondary review.
- The original paper major has 43 questions: 41 objective points and six human-reviewed writing points, total 47. The website adapts the distribution to give Concept 3 a larger share: 22 of 43 items involve transport or energy (including the writing). It retains 43 items, 41 automatically scored points and two three-point writing rubrics. The two original written prompts are adapted to respiration/photosynthesis and osmosis; protein teamwork is assessed by ordering and its guided lesson. Numbering, diagram labels and keys are intentionally different from the paper copy. The website's separate key states this clearly.
- Written explanations are saved verbatim and reviewed with a rubric. There is no keyword-based scientific grader or invented combined school grade. The displayed practice letter grade applies only to the 41 objective points.
- “No net water movement” replaces ambiguous “neither direction” wording. Motion continues at equilibrium. Model scenarios explicitly distinguish a permeable substance from a blocked one.
- Hypotonic/hypertonic are relative to the cell interior. Water questions state that water crosses and the stated non-permeating solute does not. Animal swelling/shrinking and plant turgor/plasmolysis are distinguished. Models are simplified and not to scale.
- The 20/5 and 12/12 transport setups are separate starting examples, not frames in a particle-conserving animation. A separate data table uses 24 particles throughout: 20/4, 16/8, 12/12. A 2 mL/1 mL question compares particles per mL rather than raw counts.
- Protein secretion uses ribosome → rough ER → Golgi → vesicle → release; explanatory text also acknowledges ER-to-Golgi transport vesicles.
- Plants also respire. Aerobic respiration transfers stored energy to ATP rather than creating energy. Mitochondria perform key aerobic stages; no claim is made that every respiration step occurs there.
- Chloroplasts are qualified as photosynthetic-plant structures, and lysosomes as typical animal-model structures with related plant lytic-vacuole functions. No universal “only plant/animal” organelle claim is introduced.

## Instruction and progress

Nine short mini-lessons each provide three brief facts, one worked example and 3–5 guided questions (36 total). Six primary paths focus on cell processes and the organization hierarchy; history, microscopes and organelle teamwork are secondary paths that link back to full Concepts 1/2.

The Transport Lab has fifteen starting models: five diffusion, five osmosis, three membrane-route and two vesicle scenarios. It requires a prediction before revealing feedback, offers three choices, and supports repeat exploration without penalty. Both vesicle processes have a three-step storyboard and an optional two-panel comparison.

The practice major has four choices for each multiple-choice question, visual label questions, two ordering tasks and two written explanations. Drafts and answers persist; final submission follows an answer-review screen. Test hints, correctness, writing models and the separate answer key remain hidden until submission. A new activity cannot silently discard an unfinished test, including one on its final-review screen.

First-answer evidence is immutable during practice retries. Skill counts use distinct item IDs and report assistance. A missed skill remains a suggestion until two subsequent distinct unaided successes. “Ready for a new check” is not a claim of permanent mastery. Thirty-three fresh review questions use new scenarios or numbers; exhausted pools are reported honestly.

Nine browser-local study sheets can be typed or printed; printable notes render full text rather than a clipped scrolling textarea. The separate website answer key is printable. The supplied reference PDFs/DOCX/PPTX/ZIPs are not public downloads.

## Main structures and verified distinctions

| Part | Function taught | Visual/location treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nucleus | Contains DNA; helps direct activities | Purple envelope around chromatin and nucleolus |
| Cytoplasm | Surrounds organelles; supports reactions | Material between membrane and nuclear envelope |
| Cell membrane | Controls entry and exit | Thin boundary, inside the plant wall |
| Cell wall | Shape, support and protection | Thick layer outside the plant membrane |
| Mitochondria | Transfer food energy into usable ATP | Orange shapes with inner folds, in both cells |
| Chloroplasts | Use light to make sugars | Green ovals with disk stacks in the photosynthetic plant model |
| Lysosomes | Digest and recycle materials | Animal diagram emphasis; plant vacuoles have related digestive roles |
| Ribosomes | Build proteins | Small paired dots, free or on rough ER |
| Rough ER | Processes proteins from attached ribosomes | Dotted folded membranes outside/near the nuclear envelope |
| Smooth ER | Lipid synthesis and detoxification | Tubular network without attached ribosomes |
| Golgi apparatus | Modifies, sorts and packages cargo | Separate curved stack, with nearby vesicles |
| Vacuole | Storage, especially water | Small animal sac; large central plant vacuole |
| Vesicles | Transport materials | Small membrane sacs near the Golgi |
| Cytoskeleton | Internal support and movement tracks | Thin fibers around/through the cytoplasm |

The animal model contains twelve of the fourteen main parts; the plant model thirteen. This is a teaching selection, not a claim that each real cell contains an exact organelle count. Positions and scale are schematic and variable. Label banks use at most four parts per group; introductory labeling starts with four familiar parts and intermediate labeling adds six. All main parts can be labeled and identified. Simultaneous numbered target markers are tested for non-overlap.

Six optional reference entries explain nucleolus, nuclear envelope, chromosomes, amyloplasts, centrosomes and peroxisomes. The DNA threads represent uncondensed chromatin rather than implying chromosomes are always X-shaped. The nucleolus makes rRNA and assembles ribosomal subunits. Amyloplasts store/synthesize starch in storage tissues and are not a typical feature of every leaf cell; their optional cutaway is explicitly reference material. Typical higher plant cells lack the animal centrosome with centrioles. Peroxisomes use enzymes to process fatty acids and harmful compounds, including hydrogen peroxide.

## Analogies and connected processes

Every main structure has a comparison, a shared function and a limitation. Fourteen visual models pair a cell detail with an original illustration of a familiar counterpart. Three practice directions connect object, organelle and reason. Fifteen drag/tap matching rounds cover functions, analogies and the explanations connecting them.

The school model has nine selectable areas: office/nucleus, controlled entrance/membrane, exterior wall/cell wall, power supply/mitochondria, workshop/ribosomes, mailroom/Golgi, recycling/lysosomes, storage/vacuole and framework/cytoskeleton. It is an analogy model, not a literal cell. On narrow screens selecting an area brings its one explanation into view, with a button to return to the areas.

The protein story uses the simplified stations ribosome → rough ER → Golgi → delivery vesicle. The text explicitly explains transport vesicles also move cargo between ER and Golgi; this is not taught as an uninterrupted conveyor or as the route for every protein. Subsequent questions connect nutrient entry, energy release, recycling and waste exit.

Three short function modes provide organelle-to-job, job-to-organelle and sentence completion; a fourth offers cell problems. Introductory word banks have three choices. Four paragraphs include the requested ribosome/Golgi/nucleus/membrane/mitochondria passage.

Own-analogy writing offers three comparison ideas, a custom idea field, three shared-job suggestions and an editable full explanation. Using a suggestion records assistance. Any nonblank alternative can be submitted for human/self-review; no keyword or exact-string grader claims scientific validity. Checklist/model feedback follows submission. Test writing hides these aids and remains separate from automatic points.

## Practice minor and save compatibility

New tests contain exactly **20 questions**: 5 functions, 5 highlighted-cell labels, 5 analogy reasons, 3 plant/animal classifications, and 2 written explanations. The 18 objective questions each earn one point. The two written responses await human review and contribute no automatic points. Hints, models and correctness stay hidden during an active test. Separate missed-skill counts lead to unseen review questions.

The authored bank contains 394 tasks including retained snapshots; there are 56 function tasks, 42 analogy-quest steps, 15 matching rounds, 14 writing prompts and 65 fresh review tasks. Save validation still uses the same isolated browser key. Earlier 27-question tests keep their original 24-point score and three writing responses; old labeling snapshots retain their original questions and keys. New runs use the revised curriculum. This compatibility is tested rather than requiring learners to erase earlier work.

The brief's sample “The ___ contain DNA” is grammatically mismatched for singular nucleus. The authored completion uses “The nucleus contains DNA and helps direct activities.” Plant/animal categories describe typical class models: vacuoles are in Both, lysosomal digestion is not universally animal-only, and chloroplasts occur in photosynthetic tissues rather than every plant cell. No exact official assessment, endorsement or newly verified standards alignment is claimed.

## Nine core structures and keys

| Structure | Classroom emphasis retained | Accuracy and diagram check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cell wall | Shape and support | Adds protection from the brief. Thick plant outer wall, outside the thin membrane. Animal model has no wall. Walls also occur in other groups; “plant emphasis” is a diagram category. |
| Cell membrane | Gatekeeper; controls entry and exit; semipermeable | Thin teal boundary around cytoplasm. In the plant model it is inside the green wall. “Selectively permeable” appears in optional detail. |
| Nucleus | Cell activities and DNA | Large purple structure with nuclear envelope, DNA marks, and darker nucleolus. A principal comparison refers to directing activities, rather than literal thinking. |
| Cytoplasm | Jelly-like material holding parts | Pale interior behind the other structures. Detail includes active cell processes. Selecting exposed cytoplasm targets that material, rather than a separate invented organelle. |
| Mitochondria | ATP and food breakdown | Orange beans with folded inner membranes, in both cells. Described as releasing usable energy and producing ATP, avoiding a claim that energy is created. |
| Ribosomes | Protein production | Tiny dark paired particles, distinct from the much larger pink lysosomes. Core and optional rough ER views preserve this distinction. |
| Lysosomes | Digestion and waste | Small speckled pink sacs in the animal model. The explanation includes material breakdown and reuse, with related digestive functions of plant vacuoles noted. |
| Vacuoles | Storage in both cells; much larger in plants | Blue sac in the animal cell and a large central blue plant vacuole. Water pressure helps support a plant cell; vacuoles are not sorted as absent from animals. |
| Chloroplasts | Chlorophyll and photosynthesis | Green ovals with stacks of disks, distinct from orange folded mitochondria. The model represents a photosynthetic plant cell. Optional detail includes some protists and the absence of chloroplasts from many root cells. |

The core list is the nine **named structures across the lesson**, not a graded claim that every cell has nine organelles. The class deck's “7 main / 9 for plant” count does not map cleanly to the selected core diagrams and optional structures. That count is not tested. The term “cell parts” avoids insisting that walls and cytoplasm are membrane-bound organelles.

No separate Genially URL or attachment was supplied with this update, so that exact example could not be inspected. The original click, highlight, label, and challenge behavior follows the written brief.

ER and Golgi are supported by the Unit Map and the diagrams on slides 5–6. Their folded network and separate curved stack appear only when requested or in the optional extension. This is not a full treatment of every additional structure in the broader Unit Map.

## Analogies and assessment

The lesson teaches a comparison plus an explicit shared function. The nine models, word-bank comparisons, reverse questions, and explanation choices use the same functional meanings. The principal, security gate, castle wall, power plant, factory, supporting gel, recycling center, storage tank, and light-powered food-production comparisons are teaching models; each includes a limitation. In particular, an ordinary solar panel produces electricity, whereas a chloroplast uses light energy to make sugars.

Writing accepts any nonblank text, preserves it as a draft, and never evaluates correctness by keywords or exact wording. A four-part self-review checklist asks about the named cell part, its actual job, a comparison sharing that job, and the explanation connecting them. Sample comparisons are explicitly examples, not exclusive keys. Written test answers appear separately for human review.

The practice test contains **27 tasks: 24 objective questions and 3 written analogies**. Its objective distribution is 6 diagram identifications, 7 function items (including a three-way match and a word-bank completion), 6 analogy items (including a completion), 3 plant/animal comparisons, and 2 cell-type questions. Every complete objective task is one point; a multi-blank match needs all blanks correct. Writing contributes no automatic points. Correctness and explanations remain hidden until the test is complete, and first submitted responses are immutable.

An initial analogy completion had two interchangeable blanks for support and protection. It was replaced with one blank whose answer is “support and protection,” so the system does not mark an equally valid reversed ordering wrong. Test classification items omit the guided practice's helpful context. No biological exception or ambiguous historical “first” is used as a definitive graded trap.

The short review is 12 manageable screens: four labels, four function matches, three analogy choices, and one written analogy. It has no countdown. New sessions vary the selected examples and choice order. Targeted review uses new situations, including a park entrance checkpoint, a supply warehouse, and a generating station. Shared cell parts appear in the other cell model where possible. It uses a separate pool and reports when fresh examples are exhausted; repeated practice is not represented as proof of mastery.

## Prokaryotic and eukaryotic qualifications

The original short lesson covers nucleus versus nucleoid, membrane-bound organelles, bacteria and archaea, plant/animal/fungus/protist examples, single-celled versus multicellular organisms, and the shared DNA/membrane/cytoplasm/ribosome set. The nucleoid is a region, not a membrane-bound nucleus. Ribosomes are present in prokaryotes even though they lack membrane-bound organelles.

The commercial reading's Venn key emphasizes multicellular eukaryotes, but some eukaryotes are single-celled. The new lesson states both possibilities. Its broad size claim and historical first-observation wording are outside this assessment and are not copied into graded questions. The teacher's “chloroplasts only in plants” wording is framed as typical plant/animal diagram emphasis rather than a universal claim about all organisms. Lysosomal digestion is not presented as exclusive to animals.

Independent scientific cross-checks: [OpenStax Biology 2e, Eukaryotic Cells](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells) and [Prokaryotic Cells](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-2-prokaryotic-cells). These support the clarifications; the classroom originals establish the assessment emphasis. The site makes no newly asserted official Fulton County standards-alignment claim.

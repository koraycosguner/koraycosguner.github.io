## Scientific and historical boundaries

Every image visibly carries “Conceptual historical illustration.” These scenes support a story and are not archival photographs, authentic portraits, exact instrument reconstructions, or claims about laboratory furnishings. The microscope drawings, enlarged specimens, artificial colors, and scales are illustrative.

- **Hooke:** cork view contains tan empty compartments and thick walls; no living green contents or nuclei. A code diagram explicitly identifies dead cell walls and empty compartments.
- **Leeuwenhoek:** the enlarged view combines a larger ciliate-like organism and much smaller rods/dots. Bacteria contain no drawn nuclei. It combines examples from observations across years; it does not imply all were first observed in the class timeline's 1673 date or in one exact sample. Scales are schematic.
- **Schleiden:** the leaf view has clear plant cell walls, nuclei and chloroplasts. The close-up says these are *example leaf cells* and explicitly says not every plant cell contains chloroplasts.
- **Schwann:** pink animal tissue has membranes and nuclei, with no plant cell walls or chloroplasts. The schematic uses thin, irregular boundaries rather than the thick walls of plant cells.
- **Virchow:** a parent cell leads through a two-nucleus, pinching cell to two daughter cells. The picture shows origin by division, not spontaneous appearance; it is not a full mitosis diagram or a claim that Virchow was the first person to observe division.

The teaching figures in `science/cells/CellVisual.tsx` use editable SVG and text. “Look closer” progressively reveals one concise explanation and a simplified observation diagram; this avoids competing illustrations in the default story view. Image-failure rendering supplies the same observation diagram and explanation. Images have intrinsic dimensions and descriptive alternative text; controls use native keyboard-operable details/summary.

## Optional editable worksheet

`cell-theory-worksheet.docx` and `cell-theory-answer-key.docx` are original
documents generated by `scripts/build-cells-worksheet.py`, using python-docx
from the bundled document runtime. No teacher document pages or completed learner
responses are included. The four-page worksheet contains a five-event timeline
reference, the three principles, matching, chronological ordering, a historical
word-bank passage, five multiple-choice questions, principle connections and five
spacious written-response areas. It has 27 unlocked native Word text controls;
five are extended-answer fields. The separate key is two pages.

Both documents were rendered with the documents skill's `render_docx.py` using
bundled LibreOffice. Every final page was inspected. A base-template blue title
rule was removed and both files were re-rendered. There is no clipped text,
overflowing table, or separated question/answer block in the final six pages.
A separate temporary copy was filled through a native text control, rendered,
and inspected to confirm an extended response wraps and remains editable.
The packaged accessibility audit reported no findings for the worksheet.
Desktop Microsoft Word UI editing was not exercised; OOXML controls, programmatic
filling and LibreOffice rendering were checked. The blank DOCX files alone are
included in public assets; intermediate PDFs, page images and the filled example
remain outside the repository.

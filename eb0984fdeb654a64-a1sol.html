Cost-Plus Pricing

Question 1 3 pts
Petal Pushers wants to determine the variable cost per arrangement based on the following monthly expenses: Direct Costs: $5,000 (flowers and supplies); Labor Costs: $3,000; Monthly Production Volume: 2,000 arrangements. What is the variable cost per arrangement?

Total Variable Costs = Direct Costs + Labor Costs = $5,000 + $3,000 = $8,000

Variable Cost per Arrangement = Total Variable Costs ÷ Production Volume = $8,000 ÷ 2,000 = $4

✓ Answer: $4
Question 2 3 pts
After calculating the variable cost per arrangement, Petal Pushers decides to apply a 50% markup to determine the selling price. Based on your answer to Question 1, what should the selling price be?

Variable Cost per Arrangement = $4 (from Question 1)

Markup = 50% of Variable Cost = $4 × 0.50 = $2

Selling Price = Variable Cost + Markup = $4 + $2 = $6

✓ Answer: $6
Question 4 4 pts
A microbrewery wants to charge a 50% markup on its seasonal ale, which costs $2 per bottle to produce. To attract first-time customers, they plan to sell 6-packs at a 20% discount off the single-bottle price. What will be the total price of a 6-pack after applying the discount?

Step 1 — Single-Bottle Selling Price

Cost per Bottle = $2  ·  Markup = 50%

Selling Price per Bottle = $2 + ($2 × 0.50) = $3

Step 2 — 6-Pack Price Before Discount

6-Pack Price = $3 × 6 = $18

Step 3 — Apply the 20% Discount

Discounted Price = $18 × (1 − 0.20) = $18 × 0.80 = $14.40

✓ Answer: $14.40

Economic Value to the Customer (EVC)

Question 3 4 pts
What is the EVC for EcoFlow with respect to CoolQuench (based on the provided information in Lecture 2)?

Formula

EVC = Reference Value (Price of the Competitor) + Differentiation Value

Differentiation Value Components (from Lecture 2)

Durability Value = 30 × 2 = $60

Insulation Value = 2 × (−5) = −$10

Eco-Friendly Material Value = +$10

Total Differentiation Value = $60 + (−$10) + $10 = $60

EVC = Reference Value + Differentiation Value = $60

✓ Answer: $60
Question 5 4 pts
An electric toothbrush lasts 3 years, while a competitor's model lasts only 2 years and costs $60. If customers value each extra year at $20, what is the EVC for the longer-lasting toothbrush?

Formula

EVC = Reference Value (Price of the Competitor) + Differentiation Value

Reference Value = Competitor's Price = $60

Differentiation Value = Extra years × Value per year = (3 − 2) × $20 = $20

EVC = $60 + $20 = $80

✓ Answer: $80
Question 6 4 pts
A smart thermostat helps users save $10/month in energy costs. A competing thermostat is priced at $100 with no monthly savings. What would be the EVC for the smart thermostat if the average customer uses it for 2 years?

Formula

EVC = Reference Value (Price of the Competitor) + Differentiation Value

Reference Value = Competitor's Price = $100

Differentiation Value = Monthly savings × Usage duration = $10/month × 24 months = $240

EVC = $100 + $240 = $340

✓ Answer: $340
Question 7 4 pts
A hotel charges $150 per night but includes free breakfast valued at $20 and parking at $10, which a $130 competitor lacks. What's the EVC for the hotel's offering?

Formula

EVC = Reference Value (Price of the Competitor) + Differentiation Value

Reference Value = Competitor's Price = $130

Differentiation Value = Breakfast ($20) + Parking ($10) = $30

EVC = $130 + $30 = $160

Note: The hotel's actual price ($150) is below its EVC ($160), meaning the customer captures $10 in surplus — a deliberate strategy to drive demand.

✓ Answer: $160

Pricing Leverage & Profit Impact

Question 8 3 pts
A company sells 10,000 units/month. Selling Price: $100; Variable Cost: $90; Fixed Costs: $80,000/month. If the company increases its selling price by 1%, what would be the percentage profit increase, assuming units sold remain the same?

Original Profit

Revenue = 10,000 × $100 = $1,000,000

Total Cost = (10,000 × $90) + $80,000 = $980,000

Profit = $1,000,000 − $980,000 = $20,000

Profit After 1% Price Increase

New Price = $100 × 1.01 = $101

New Revenue = 10,000 × $101 = $1,010,000

New Profit = $1,010,000 − $980,000 = $30,000

Percentage Increase

% Increase = ($30,000 − $20,000) / $20,000 × 100 = 50%

This illustrates the powerful leverage of pricing on profit: when margins are thin, even a small percentage increase in price translates into a disproportionately large increase in profit.

✓ Answer: 50%

Pricing Strategy Concepts

Question 9 3 pts · True / False
Premium pricing and luxury pricing both rely on artificial scarcity to justify high prices.

False. These are distinct strategies. Luxury pricing relies on artificial scarcity and exclusivity (e.g., Ferrari limits production to preserve brand cachet). Premium pricing relies on superior quality, innovation, or ecosystem value and can scale broadly (e.g., Apple charges a premium but produces at massive volume). Artificial scarcity is central to luxury pricing but is not a defining feature of premium pricing.

✓ Answer: False
Question 10 3 pts · True / False
A company "stuck in the middle" (on the price positioning spectrum) fails because it tries to serve too many customer segments without committing to a clear positioning.

True. A firm that is "stuck in the middle" lacks a clear value proposition: it is neither the lowest-cost option nor a clearly differentiated premium or luxury offering. Without committed positioning, the firm struggles to attract any segment consistently — price-sensitive customers find cheaper alternatives, while quality-seeking customers find more differentiated competitors.

✓ Answer: True
Question 11 3 pts · Multiple Choice
Which of the following statements about value pricing are true?

I. Value pricing means offering the lowest price in the market through cost leadership.
II. Value pricing requires process innovation that reduces costs without sacrificing quality.
III. Customers in the value segment typically accept some trade-offs, such as self-service or DIY assembly, in exchange for lower prices.

I — False. Value pricing does not necessarily mean the absolute lowest price. It means offering a strong price-to-quality ratio, often enabled by cost innovation rather than by simply being the cheapest.

II — True. Value pricing is sustained through process innovations — supply chain efficiencies, automation, simplified product design — that reduce costs while maintaining acceptable quality.

III — True. Value-segment customers knowingly accept trade-offs (e.g., self-service at IKEA, no-frills experience at Southwest Airlines) in exchange for lower prices.

✓ Answer: II and III
Question 12 3 pts · Multiple Choice
Which of the following statements about price elasticity are true?

I. A price elasticity of −0.5 means demand is inelastic and the firm has pricing power.
II. A price elasticity of −2.0 means a 5% price increase would lead to approximately a 10% drop in quantity demanded.
III. Products with many close substitutes tend to have inelastic demand.

I — True. An elasticity of −0.5 (|e| < 1) indicates inelastic demand: quantity demanded is relatively unresponsive to price changes, giving the firm pricing power.

II — True. By definition, elasticity = % change in quantity ÷ % change in price. With e = −2.0, a 5% price increase yields approximately a 10% decrease in quantity demanded.

III — False. Products with many close substitutes tend to have elastic demand (|e| > 1) because customers can easily switch to alternatives when prices rise.

✓ Answer: I and II
Question 13 3 pts · Multiple Choice
Which of the following statements about the price waterfall are true?

I. The pocket price is what a firm actually collects after all discounts, allowances, and concessions.
II. Most companies lose money between list price and pocket price without realizing it.
III. The solution to pricing leaks is to lower the list price so there is less room to discount.

I — True. The pocket price represents the actual revenue collected per unit after subtracting all on-invoice discounts and off-invoice concessions (payment terms, freight, co-op advertising, etc.).

II — True. A key insight from the price waterfall framework is that firms often have significant, unmonitored revenue leakage between list price and pocket price, arising from accumulated discounts managed by different departments.

III — False. Lowering the list price does not address the root cause of pricing leaks. The solution is to audit and manage each element of the waterfall — identifying where margin is lost and establishing controls to prevent unnecessary discounting.

✓ Answer: I and II
Question 14 3 pts · Multiple Choice
Which of the following statements are true?

I. Ferrari and Apple both charge high prices, but Ferrari's pricing power would be destroyed by scaling up while Apple's pricing power depends on it.
II. If Ferrari doubled production tomorrow, its profit margin would likely increase because it would sell twice as many cars.
III. Apple's 90% customer retention rate is a symptom of inelastic demand created by ecosystem switching costs.

I — True. Ferrari's pricing power derives from exclusivity and artificial scarcity — scaling up would erode the brand's luxury positioning. Apple's pricing power is reinforced by network effects and ecosystem lock-in, which strengthen as scale increases.

II — False. Doubling production would undermine Ferrari's exclusivity. Prices and margins would likely fall because the brand's luxury cachet depends on constrained availability.

III — True. Apple's high retention rate reflects inelastic demand driven by switching costs. Customers embedded in the Apple ecosystem (iCloud, apps, peripherals, cross-device integration) face substantial costs if they switch, making them less sensitive to price increases.

✓ Answer: I and III
Question 15 3 pts · Multiple Choice
Consider two companies: Company A has a QEAT score of −0.4 and Company B has a QEAT score of −3.2. Which of the following are true?

I. Company A has more pricing power than Company B.
II. Company A could likely raise prices with relatively little loss in volume.
III. Company B should invest in building switching costs or differentiation before attempting a price increase.

I — True. A QEAT score closer to zero (|e| < 1) indicates inelastic demand and therefore greater pricing power. Company A's score of −0.4 reflects far less price sensitivity than Company B's −3.2.

II — True. With a QEAT of −0.4, a price increase would produce only a modest decline in quantity demanded, allowing Company A to raise prices with relatively little volume loss.

III — True. With a QEAT of −3.2, Company B faces highly elastic demand — even small price increases would trigger large volume losses. Before raising prices, Company B should invest in differentiation or switching costs to reduce price sensitivity.

✓ Answer: I, II, and III

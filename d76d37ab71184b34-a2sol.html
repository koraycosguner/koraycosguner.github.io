Question 1 5 pts
What is the interpretation of the estimated intercept in Coke's weekly sales regression?

Interpretation

The intercept (β₀ = 1,194.99) represents the predicted value of SalesCoke when PriceCoke = 0. In other words, if Coke were free, the model predicts approximately 1,195 units would be sold per week.

Note: This is a mathematical interpretation. A price of $0 is outside the observed data range, so the intercept does not have a practical real-world meaning — it simply anchors the regression line.

✓ Answer: When the price of Coke is $0, the predicted sales of Coke is about 1195 units.
Question 2 5 pts · True / False
The estimated slope in Coke's weekly sales regression could be interpreted as follows: "One unit increase in the price of Coke increases the predicted sales of Coke by 500 units."

FALSE. The slope coefficient is negative (β₁ = −500.27), so a one-dollar increase in the price of Coke decreases predicted weekly sales by about 500 units — it does not increase them. The statement gets the direction wrong.

✓ Answer: FALSE
Question 3 5 pts · True / False
The R² in Coke's weekly sales regression could be interpreted as follows: "26.4% of the variation in the price of Coke could be explained by the sales of Coke."

FALSE. The statement reverses the dependent and independent variables. R² describes how much variation in the dependent variable (SalesCoke) is explained by the independent variable (PriceCoke), not the other way around. The correct interpretation: 26.4% of the variation in the sales of Coke is explained by the price of Coke.

✓ Answer: FALSE
Question 4 5 pts
What is the predicted sales of Coke when the price of Coke is $2?

Calculation

SalesCoke = 1,194.99 + (−500.27) × PriceCoke

SalesCoke = 1,194.99 − 500.27 × 2

SalesCoke = 1,194.99 − 1,000.54 = 194.44

✓ Answer: 194.44
Question 5 5 pts
If the wholesale price of Coke is $1 (i.e., the cost to FreshMart), what is the profit-maximizing retail price for Coke?

Note: Use the range of 1 to 3 for the possible range of optimal prices.

Logic

Profit = (Price − Cost) × Predicted Sales = (P − 1) × (1,194.99 − 500.27 · P)

We need to find the price P that maximizes this profit function. There are three ways to do this.

Method 1 — Excel

After running your Coke regression, copy just the coefficients into a clean area and set up the following:

A B C
1 Coefficients
2 Intercept 1194.986705
3 Price_Coke -500.2726941
4
5 Price 1.694
6 Sales_Coke 347.357 =B2+B3*B5
7 Profit_Coke 241.182 =(B5-1)*B6

Use Solver: set B7 (Profit) as the objective to maximize, with B5 (Price) as the decision variable, constrained between 1 and 3. Solver returns P* = 1.694.

Alternatively, you can discretize the price between 1 and 3 in $0.01 increments, compute the profit at each price, and see which one gives the highest profit.

Method 2 — R

profit_Coke = function(P) {
  sales = 1194.99 - 500.27 * P
  profit = (P - 1) * sales
  return(profit)
}

optimize(profit_Coke, c(1, 3), maximum = TRUE)
# $maximum = 1.694    $objective = 241.18

Method 3 — Tool 8: General Purpose Optimizer

Enter the following equations:

Sales_Coke = 1194.99 - 500.27 * Price_Coke
Profit_Coke = (Price_Coke - 1) * Sales_Coke

Set Maximize: Profit_Coke. Choose Price_Coke as the decision variable with bounds 1 to 3. Leave Control Variables empty. Click Find Optimal Solution.

The tool returns: Price_Coke = 1.694, Profit_Coke = 241.18.

Result

At P* = 1.694: Sales ≈ 347.36, Profit = (1.694 − 1) × 347.36 ≈ $241.18

✓ Answer: 1.694

Question 6 5 pts · True / False
The estimated intercept in Pepsi's weekly sales regression could be interpreted as follows: "When the price of Pepsi is zero, the predicted sales for Pepsi becomes 1268 units."

TRUE. The intercept (β₀ = 1,268.00) is the predicted value of SalesPepsi when PricePepsi = 0. The statement correctly describes this mathematical interpretation: if the price of Pepsi were $0, the model predicts approximately 1,268 units sold per week.

✓ Answer: TRUE
Question 7 5 pts · True / False
The estimated slope in Pepsi's weekly sales regression could be interpreted as follows: "When the price of Pepsi increases by 1 unit, the predicted sales of Pepsi decreases by 1268 units."

FALSE. The statement confuses the intercept with the slope. The slope coefficient is β₁ = −540.69, not −1,268. The correct interpretation: when the price of Pepsi increases by $1, the predicted sales of Pepsi decreases by approximately 541 units. The value 1,268 is the intercept, not the slope.

✓ Answer: FALSE
Question 8 5 pts · True / False
The R² in Pepsi's weekly sales regression could be interpreted as follows: "25.6% of the variation in the sales of Pepsi could be explained by the price of Pepsi."

TRUE. This correctly states that the R² (0.2564) measures the proportion of variation in the dependent variable (SalesPepsi) that is explained by the independent variable (PricePepsi). Unlike the Coke version of this question (Q3), the direction is stated correctly here — sales is the dependent variable and price is the independent variable.

✓ Answer: TRUE
